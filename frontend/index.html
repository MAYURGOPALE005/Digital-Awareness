Why Digital Privacy Matters

In today's connected world, your personal data is one of your most valuable assets. Here's why protecting it matters.

Protect Personal Information

Your name, phone number, and personal details can be misused if they fall into the wrong hands. Keep them safe.

Secure Online Accounts

Strong passwords and two-factor authentication help keep your email, banking, and social media accounts safe.

Prevent Digital Fraud

Awareness about common scams like phishing, fake links, and payment fraud can save you from financial loss.

Control Your Digital Footprint

Everything you do online leaves a trace. Learn to manage what information you share publicly.

Common Digital Privacy Risks

Being aware of these common risks is the first step to staying safe online.

Phishing

Fake emails or messages that trick you into sharing personal information or clicking harmful links.

Weak Passwords

Simple or reused passwords make it easy for attackers to break into your accounts.

Unsafe Public Wi-Fi

Unsecured networks can allow others to see your online activity and steal your data.

Excessive App Permissions

Some apps request access to your camera, contacts, or location without really needing it.

Social Media Oversharing

Sharing too much personal information publicly can put your safety and privacy at risk.

Online Payment Fraud

Fake payment requests, malicious QR codes, and OTP scams can lead to financial loss.

Quick Privacy Tips

Simple steps you can take right now to improve your digital privacy.

1

Use a strong, unique password for every important account. Consider a password manager.

2

Turn on Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) wherever possible for extra security.

3

Never share your OTP, UPI PIN, or banking passwords with anyone — even if they claim to be from your bank.

4

Think before you click — verify links and sender details before clicking on anything suspicious.

5

Review and remove unnecessary app permissions on your phone regularly.

6

Avoid using public Wi-Fi for banking, shopping, or entering passwords.
