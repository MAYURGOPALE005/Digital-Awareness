Learn Digital Privacy

Understand the key topics of digital privacy and cybersecurity in simple language. Each section includes practical tips you can use right away.

Password Security

Your password is the first line of defense for your online accounts. A weak password is like leaving your front door unlocked.

  • Use a strong password — at least 12 characters with a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols.
  • Use a unique password for every important account (email, banking, social media).
  • Consider using a password manager like Google Password Manager or Bitwarden to store your passwords safely.
  • Never share your passwords with anyone, not even friends or family.
Safety Tip

A good password could be a phrase you can remember, like "MyDog$Loves2Run!" — it's long, unique, and hard to guess.

Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

Two-Factor Authentication adds an extra layer of protection to your account. Even if someone knows your password, they cannot get in without the second step.

  • What is 2FA? After entering your password, you also need a second verification — like an OTP sent to your phone or a code from an authenticator app.
  • Why it matters: If your password is stolen, 2FA stops attackers from accessing your account.
  • Examples: OTP on SMS, Google Authenticator, fingerprint or face unlock on banking apps.
Safety Tip

Turn on 2FA for your email, social media, and banking apps. It takes just a minute to set up but adds strong protection.

Phishing Attacks

Phishing is when attackers send fake emails, messages, or create fake websites to trick you into sharing personal information like passwords or bank details.

  • Common warning signs: Urgent language ("Your account will be blocked!"), spelling mistakes, and unfamiliar sender addresses.
  • Suspicious links: Hover over links before clicking. If the URL looks strange or doesn't match the official website, don't click it.
  • Fake messages: Messages claiming you've won a prize, asking you to verify your account, or requesting OTPs are usually scams.
Safety Tip

When in doubt, go directly to the official website by typing the address yourself instead of clicking on links in messages.

Social Media Privacy

Social media is fun, but sharing too much personal information can put your safety at risk.

  • Privacy settings: Review and adjust who can see your posts, photos, and profile information.
  • Oversharing: Avoid posting personal details like your home address, phone number, daily schedule, or travel plans.
  • Location sharing: Turn off location tags on posts and check which apps have access to your location.
  • Public profiles: If your profile is public, anyone can see your information. Switch to a private profile if possible.
Safety Tip

Before posting something, ask yourself: "Would I be comfortable if a stranger saw this?" If not, keep it private.

App Permissions

When you install an app, it may ask for permission to access your camera, microphone, location, contacts, or storage. Not all apps need all these permissions.

  • Camera & Microphone: Only allow access when the app genuinely needs it (e.g., a video calling app).
  • Location: Many apps track your location in the background. Allow only when necessary and choose "While using the app."
  • Contacts & Storage: Be cautious about apps that want access to your contact list or files without a clear reason.
Safety Tip

Go to your phone settings and review app permissions regularly. Remove permissions that an app doesn't need.

Online Payment Safety

Digital payments through UPI, net banking, and wallets are convenient — but you need to stay alert against fraud.

  • OTP safety: Your OTP is a secret code. No bank, company, or government agency will ever ask for it.
  • UPI PIN safety: Your UPI PIN is like your ATM PIN — never share it. You only enter it to send money, never to receive.
  • Fake payment requests: Scammers may send "collect" requests pretending to be refunds. Always verify before approving.
  • Suspicious QR codes: Don't scan random QR codes. Scanning a QR code can be used to take money from your account, not send it to you.
Safety Tip

Remember: You never need to enter your UPI PIN or share an OTP to receive money. If someone asks you to, it's a scam.
